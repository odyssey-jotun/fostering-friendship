About Fostering Friendships

Our Mission

To create peer-to-peer friendships with children in foster care and those at risk of going into foster care, to reduce stigma, ease anxiety, provide fun and joy, and create friendship and equality between such children and others not in the foster system, and spread this mission across the country and the globe.

What We Do

Today, in my community, statewide communities, and nationwide communities, foster children and many at risk of going into foster care are stigmatized, defined only by their past trauma, and excluded from having friends and relationships in the world outside of their foster homes. Social exclusionary practices are created and re-created by several structural mechanisms, such as cultural norms and discriminatory practices(Frisby and Ponic, 2013), but often also due to a lack of access. As a volunteer for the Centers for Youth and Family, one of the most extensive foster care programs in Arkansas, I have seen firsthand the effects of such social exclusion and stigma on these children.

These children, no different than me or you, are excluded from the joy and benefits of friendship simply because of their past. Today and in the days to come, Fostering Friendships stands to eliminate the stigma surrounding foster children by breaking down structural barriers to social inclusion, creating peer-to-peer friendships with such children and those not in the foster system, and building friendship and equality.

Our Values: Friendship, Inclusion, and Equality

Building Friendships and Inclusion: Building friendships and equality between people can be as easy as hanging out for a couple of hours, but genuine bonds that foster change form through commonplace. The word commonplace has many definitions. However, I define it as a shared experience, activity, or thought that brings people together.

Such commonplaces include sports such as basketball games, board and card games, or something as simple as a magic trick. Commonplace embodies the goal of Fostering Friendships, and therefore, to achieve our goal, we bring people together from entirely different backgrounds and connect them through commonplace.

  • Friendship

    Genuine bonds that foster change, formed through commonplace.

  • Inclusion

    Bringing people together from entirely different backgrounds.

  • Equality

    Friendship and equality between such children and others not in the foster system.

Acting President

Keene Jones

I am Keene Jones, Jackson’s sister. I helped him start Fostering Friendships in 2024, and I am the Acting President while he is away at school. The day to day is mine now: finding the centers and the sponsors, putting the events together, and making sure the friendships that start at one of them are still there a month later.

I also teach movement seminars, which means I spend most of my week in a room full of people who had counted themselves out before they ever walked in. It is the same thing the research on this site keeps finding. A basketball game, a card game, a magic trick, a class where nobody is keeping score: the activity is almost beside the point. What matters is that it puts two people in the same room with something to share, and that is where a friendship gets to start.

Research

Social inclusion aims to create fair and equitable opportunities for everyone. The consequence of it enables underserved and neglected groups to participate in various activities, such as sports and recreation. Our mission aims to understand, produce, and develop research based practices that enhance quality of life by fostering community acceptance, belonging, and trust, especially with at risk foster kids. This is why we based all of our approaches from peer reviewed research with this precious demographic.
